Why teams use StepUp for workplace challenges

StepUp has been used for step challenges by teams at companies including Amazon, BMW and Google. It's built to be the simplest way to run one.

Free for everyone

No per-employee fees, no contract and no features behind a paywall, so you can invite the whole company without a budget request.

Set up in minutes

No IT integration or HR system needed. Create a group for up to 15,000 people, share the invite link, and people join from their own phones.

Any phone, any wearable

Works on iPhone and Android with just a phone in a pocket, and syncs Apple Watch, Fitbit, Garmin, Samsung and more.

Privacy-friendly

Only group members see each other's steps. StepUp doesn't use GPS location, doesn't sell data, and anyone can delete their account at any time.

Friendly, not stressful

Mystery Mode hides today's step counts and shows only ranks, and admins can turn off chat or taunts if they'd rather keep things low-key.

Works across offices

Steps count in each person's local time zone, and you can run a group per office or team to compare results across locations.

Plan your workplace step challenge

A simple timeline that works for most teams, from a single department to a whole company.

  1. 2 weeks before

    Pick your dates and format: company-wide, team vs team, or ongoing monthly. Decide how you'll recognise people and get a manager or two to join in.

  2. 1 week before

    Create your group (or one per team), set custom leaderboard dates, and send the invite email so everyone can install StepUp in time.

  3. During the challenge

    Send a short weekly update, cheer people on in group chat, and give shout-outs for effort as well as top spots.

  4. After it ends

    Export the group report, announce winners and team totals, and keep the group going for an ongoing monthly challenge.

Workplace step challenge ideas

Walktober or a 30-day challenge

Set custom leaderboard dates for a month and see who walks the most. October, January and spring are popular times to start.

Office vs office

Create one group per office, region or department and compare average daily steps per member using exported reports. Great for distributed companies.

Everyone's a winner

Instead of rewarding only the top step count, enter everyone who hits a goal, such as 7,000 steps a day, into a prize draw.

Steps for charity

Turn the company's combined steps into a donation, such as a set amount for every million steps, using the group's total steps from the exported report.

Walking meetings month

Pair the challenge with walking one-to-ones and lunchtime walks so people can build steps into the working day.

Mystery Mode month

Turn on Mystery Mode so only ranks are visible during the day, with step counts revealed the next morning. It keeps everyone guessing until the end.

Keep it fair and inclusive

Make it optional

Step challenges work best when people choose to join. Invite everyone, but make it clear that taking part is voluntary and that there's no pressure to share steps with colleagues.

Compare teams by average, not total

When teams have different sizes, compare average daily steps per member rather than total steps, so a small team has the same chance as a large one.

Reward effort as well as results

Prizes only for the most steps can discourage everyone else. Recognise people who hit a personal goal, improve the most, or keep a streak going, alongside the top spots.

Plan for different abilities

Not everyone can take part in a step challenge in the same way. Goal-based prize draws and team averages help, and it's worth offering other ways to join in, such as recognising time spent being active.

Steps are counted automatically

StepUp doesn't allow manually added steps, so everyone's count comes from their phone or wearable. That keeps leaderboards fair without anyone needing to police them.
